INTRODUCTION

1. Logograms

1-1. Selection of logograms

We have excerpted 13 logograms (plus 1 variant form) from approximately 330 logograms (plus about 50 variant forms) enlisted in the Japan Cetkaik Association’s Essential Dictionary of Lanermic Logograms. This amounts to 4% of the original.

1-2. Sorting method

Since this dictionary is an abridgement from Japan Cetkaik Association’s Essential Dictionary of Lanermic Logograms, which in turn lists logograms in the 二引形撃字機 order, the ordering is completely inherited from the original dictionary.

1-3. English gloss

To foster familiarity with Lanermic logograms, we have provided English glosses for each logograms appearing in the dictionary. To give an example, 木紙遊術 hup1 nin1 tu2 sit1 ‘board game’ is denoted as【TREE : PAPER : GAME : METHOD】, roughly approximating what each logogram means through English words.

1-4. Pekzep readings and how they are denoted

While Pekzep isn’t the only language that uses Lanermic logograms, and while Pekzep is not a language that solely uses Lanermic logograms, the practice of reading Lanermic logogram sequences aloud in Pekzep phonetics without interpreting parts of speech is widespread. Therefore, for characters with stable conventional Pekzep readings, those readings are provided.

2. Parts of speech

Each headword lists its parts of speech based on how it is used in the Pekzep language. Due to the fact that words hardly inflect in Pekzep, theories regarding parts of speech remain vague; however, for convenience, we have established the following criteria.

2-1. What ‘falls short’ of being a word

We have recognised three types of elements that cannot appear independently within a sentence as standalone components: abbreviations, prefixes, and suffixes.

2-2. A word

Words denoting persons, things, and so forth, along with words exhibiting similar syntactic behaviour, are classified as nouns. Words denoting actions, states, and so forth, which form the core of a sentence, are classified as verbs. Among these, those exhibiting a strong tendency to express states and which, syntactically, particularly resists taking direct objects or the progressive marker 在 are specifically classified as stative verbs. Furthermore, verbs that take clauses as their direct objects are classified as clause-taking verbs. Words uttered to express emotion directly, rather than appearing within other sentences, are classified as interjections. Those with a strong tendency to modify the immediately following noun syntactically and semantically are classified as prenominals for convenience. Words serving to connect sentences are classified as conjunctions for sentences.

2-3. Divisible compound words

Numerous instances exist where multiple characters combine to form a single meaning, yet the constituent characters do not necessarily appear consecutively within the text, allowing other words to intervene between them. Whilst it would have been possible to adopt a policy of not recognising these as words and omitting them from the dictionary, grasping these kinds of ‘divisible compound words’ is indispensable for a thorough linguistic understanding of how sentences written in Lanermic logograms behave. We therefore endeavoured to include them proactively. Where each separable part can be clearly identified as a constituent element within a sentence, it is denoted as such, e.g., [verb + object].

3. Entries

Where a word has several meanings, they are grouped under the same headword and arranged as separate entries. Each entry is identified by its part of speech, and substantially different senses are distinguished accordingly.

Supplementary explanations regarding the semantics of words are indicated in parentheses ( ). Where additional information concerning the frequency of the word itself in Pekzep or syntactic constraints is necessary, this is indicated in curly brackets { }. Furthermore, additional explanations have been provided on new lines wherever deemed necessary.

4. Example sentences

Example sentences have been provided for some headwords to concisely illustrate the usage of words.
