INTRODUCTION

1. Headwords

We have excerpted 45 entries from the 914 words listed in the Japan Cetkaik Association’s Essential Dictionary of Pemecepe Creole. This amounts to more than 4% of the original.

Rather than uncritically acknowledging the existence of ‘e form’, ‘it form’, and ‘leti form’ for every conceivable verb, we strived to record words based on attestation: for instance, if it is overwhelmingly common to use a word in its transitive verb form, such a word may be enlisted only in its predominant form.

2. Sorting

To maximise convenience for English speakers, the entries have been arranged in alphabetical order based on their Latin alphabet transcription, instead of the standard ordering p b m c x z t d n l k a e i u o j w.

3. Parts of speech

Based on the analysis in jo lijuit kije pemecepe lukup leti lata leti kulante (The Book for Those Who Learn Pemecepe Creole), we have classified words of Pemecepe Creole into twelve parts of speech: noun, verb, special verb, adjective, preverbal auxiliary verb, postverbal auxiliary verb, preposition, interjection, numeral, conjunction for sentences, conjunction for nouns and special conjunction.

Verbs that end with it and take an object are denoted as ‘transitive verbs’. What is usually referred to as ‘leti form’ is enlisted as a sub-entry if its use is especially frequent; we treat such an entry as an adjective. The suffixes that form nouns when attached to nouns are indicated as ‘noun suffixes’.

4. Entries

Where a word has several meanings, they are grouped under the same headword and arranged as separate entries. Each entry is identified by its part of speech, and substantially different senses are distinguished accordingly.
